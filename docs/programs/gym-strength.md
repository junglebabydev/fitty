# Gym Strength — research and programme (Oct 2026)

**Verdict.** The core of a beginner gym programme rests on strong evidence: 2–3 sessions a week [1][2][3], working up to about 10 sets per muscle per week [2], with more sets building more muscle [4][5], and sets stopped short of failure [2][9]. Progression has support [1][17], and so does repeating the same leg lifts rather than rotating them [14]. Most of the exact numbers (rep ranges, rest times, the easier week, the week-by-week effort targets) are conventions inside a well-supported range, and this doc labels them as such. The thing most apps get wrong is constant novelty: a new workout every day feels fresh, but random, frequent exercise changes may blunt gains [13], while repeating the same leg lifts worked as well as varying them in a 10-week trial [14].

## 1. Who it is for, and who should check first

- **For:**
  - Adults 18+ with gym access who lift less than once a week now, or have not lifted for 6+ months.
  - People who did some lifting before and want a structured restart. ACSM calls someone with about 6 months of consistent training "intermediate"; before that, or after several years off, they count as "novice" [1].
  - Goal: get stronger and add some muscle. The programme is not built for a powerlifting meet, a sport or a race.
  - Users with a knee, hip, back or neck condition flag. They get the named modified paths in §4.
- **Not for, or talk to a clinician first:**
  - Anyone told by a doctor they have a heart condition or high blood pressure, or anyone with chest pain at rest or during activity. The PAR-Q+ asks about both [24]; requiring clearance first is our stricter rule.
  - Anyone who has fainted or lost balance from dizziness in the last 12 months. A PAR-Q+ question [24]; requiring clearance first is our rule.
  - Anyone told to exercise only under medical supervision (a PAR-Q+ question [24]). Do not start.
  - Pregnant users. Talk to their midwife or doctor first [24]; the app does not start this series.
  - Anyone who gave birth in the last 12 months (a coaching-convention cut-off). Route to the Postpartum series first, unless they have already finished it; Postpartum hands its graduates to this series.
  - Anyone with a current bone, joint or soft-tissue problem that limits activity now (a PAR-Q+ question [24]). Run the symptom check-in first; if it gates RED, do not start that region's work.
  - Anyone ill today (cold, fever). Wait until they feel better [24].
  - People under 18. Out of scope for this series.
  - Experienced lifters (2+ years of consistent training). They will outgrow the rep ranges and volumes in a few weeks. Not unsafe, just not the right series.

**Dose (sets per week).** Muscle growth rises with weekly sets. Schoenfeld's 2017 meta-regression found each added weekly set raised the average gain by about 0.37% [4]. The 2026 meta-regressions by Pelland and colleagues (67 studies, 2,058 people) confirm that size and strength rise with volume, with diminishing returns. The returns diminish faster for strength than for size [5]. The 2026 ACSM position stand, an overview of 137 systematic reviews, found that 10 or more sets per muscle per week enhanced size [2]. Phase 1 sits at about 7–11 sets for the large muscles; phase 2 reaches about 9–12. The table in §3 shows the count, with indirect sets counted as half, the method [5] found fitted best.

**Frequency.** A 2016 meta-analysis of 10 studies found twice a week beat once a week for size [6]. The larger 2019 update (25 studies) found that, with volume matched, frequency did not meaningfully change size [7], and the 2026 ACSM stand agrees [2]. So for size, weekly sets matter more than how they are spread. For strength, higher frequency helped, but mostly because it allowed more total volume [8]; the 2026 meta-regression found a real but diminishing strength benefit of frequency [5]. Every large muscle is trained in both session A and session B, so the 3-day track trains each one 3 times a week. The 4-day option trains each muscle less often (twice a week) but gives it more sets per session.

**Intensity and effort.** For beginners, ACSM's 2009 stand recommends 8–12 RM loads [1]. The 2026 stand says sets do not need to go to failure; a target of 2–3 reps in reserve gives enough effort, though it notes exact targets are not yet settled. Strength gains favour heavier loads (80% of max or more) [2]. Refalo's 2023 meta-analysis found no clear size advantage for momentary failure [9]. Robinson's 2024 meta-regressions suggest size improves as sets end closer to failure, while strength does not change much, but the authors call this exploratory [10]. In one small study, novice squatters rated a true 1RM as less hard than experienced lifters did [20]. Our inference (not tested) is that new lifters misjudge how close they are to failure, so we start at 3 reps in reserve and only move to 1–2 once the lifts are learned. Phase 2 drops the main-lift range to 6–10 for a little more load, a step towards the heavier loads that [2] links to strength.

**Progression.** ACSM's rule is to add 2–10% load once you beat the target by 1–2 reps [1]. Our double progression follows it: work up the rep range, then add load. An 8-week RCT in trained lifters found that adding reps at a fixed load worked about as well as adding load [17], so the "reps first" half of the rule is not a compromise. Keeping the same lifts for 6 weeks at a time is supported: a 10-week RCT in 70 young women found virtually identical size and strength gains with constant or rotating leg exercises [14]. It tested leg lifts only. The 2022 systematic review adds that random, high-frequency rotation may hurt [13]. The planned easier week is expert consensus, not trial evidence [19]. The one RCT we found tested a full week off (not a lighter week) in trained lifters. It found no size benefit and smaller lower-body strength gains [18]. So our easier week keeps every session and cuts sets.

**Rest.** Singer's 2024 Bayesian meta-analysis found a small size benefit for rests over 60 s and little difference past 90 s [16]. An 8-week RCT in 21 trained men found 3 min beat 1 min for strength and thigh size [15]. The 2026 ACSM stand found rest under or over 1 min did not change strength gains, and had too little data to judge size [2]. We use 120 s on main lifts and 60–90 s on smaller lifts. That stays inside the evidence and keeps sessions at about 45–55 minutes.

**Expected results and timescale.** Trials of 6–52 weeks show clear gains in strength, size and physical function versus doing nothing [2]. Across 111 studies in healthy men, average whole-body lean-mass gain was about 1.5 kg [22]. Individual response varies a lot. In a 12-week arm-training study of 585 adults, biceps size changed by −2% to +59% and 1RM by 0 to +250%, and women gained more relative strength than men [21]. App copy should say: expect your lifts to go up over the 12 weeks, expect size to change more slowly, and expect your result to differ from someone else's. No kilogram promises.

## 3. The programme

- **Length:** 12 weeks. **Sessions:** 3 a week (weeks 1–6). Weeks 7–12: stay on 3, or move to a 4-day upper/lower split if earned (rule P7).
- **Minutes:** about 44–46 in weeks 1 and 6, 53–55 in the build weeks, up to about 60 if the weeks 9–11 extra sets are earned. The times come from a script: 5 min bike warm-up, 1 min set-up per exercise, 90 s per ramp-up set, about 4 s per rep, and the listed rests.
- **Equipment:** leg press, cable stack (pulldown, row, pushdown, Pallof), dumbbells, adjustable bench, leg-curl machine, stationary bike. Barbell, trap bar and calf machines are optional (phase 2 and the 4-day track).
- **Session shape:** `sets` only (sets × reps, plus timed holds). One `steady` warm-up block per session (5 min easy bike). No interval or circuit blocks: this series is about load progression, not conditioning.
- **Order:** sessions alternate A and B. Odd weeks run A-B-A; even weeks B-A-B. Leave at least one day between sessions (convention).
- **Standalone picks** (for the all-workouts page): `w2d2` as "Full Body A" (54 min) and `w2d1` as "Full Body B" (53 min), both machines and dumbbells. Both still run the pre-start screen and the condition-flag swaps in §4.

Phase 1 is well above the smallest doses shown to raise strength (one set per exercise, 1–3 times a week) [11][12] and below the ≥10-set level [2] for most muscles. That is deliberate: weeks 1–5 are for learning the lifts. Phase 2 reaches about 9–12 for the large muscles. Arms get less direct work on the 3-day track. That is a trade-off for session length, not an oversight.

## 4. Safety

**Pre-start screen.** Seven yes/no questions, in our own words, based on the themes of the PAR-Q+ [24] and the ACSM screening model [23].

| # | Question | If "yes", the app… |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Has a doctor told you that you have a heart condition or high blood pressure, or do you get chest pain at rest, in daily life or when active? | Asks them to get clearance from a doctor first. Does not start the series. Shows a "Got clearance" button that unlocks it. |
| 2 | In the last 12 months, have you fainted, or lost your balance because of dizziness? | Same as 1: clearance first, do not start. |
| 3 | Has a doctor said you should only exercise under medical supervision? | Does not start the series. Shows "This series is unsupervised, so it isn't the right fit right now." No unlock button. |
| 4 | Do you have a bone, joint or muscle problem (knee, hip, back, neck or shoulder) that limits you right now? | Runs the symptom check-in for that region. AMBER: sets the condition flag and shows the modified path below. RED: does not start; suggests getting it assessed. |
| 5a | Are you pregnant? | `wait`. Asks them to check with their midwife or doctor and does not start this series. |
| 5b | Have you had a baby in the last 12 months? | `suggest:postpartum`: offers the Postpartum series instead and does not start this one. Exception: if the user's Postpartum enrolment has `status: 'done'`, the answer is noted and this series starts as normal, because Postpartum hands its graduates here. The 12-month cut-off is a coaching convention. |
| 6 | Do you feel unwell today, such as a cold or fever? | Does not start today. Offers to start tomorrow. |

Note for question 1: the PAR-Q+ also advises people over 45 who are not used to hard exercise to talk to a qualified exercise professional before vigorous or maximal effort [24]. This series never asks for a maximal lift, and RIR targets never go below 1.

**Stop signs during or after a session.** Exercise-related cardiac events are often preceded by warning signs [23]. The NHS lists fainting while exercising [25] and a headache that starts suddenly and is extremely painful [26] as reasons to call emergency services. The other rows and their actions are a safety convention, not a tested protocol. Emergency wording matches `medical_emergency` in `chatSafety.ts`.

| Sign | What the app does |
|---|---|
| Chest pain, pressure or tightness; pain spreading to arm, jaw or back | Stop the session. Sit down. If it does not ease within a few minutes of rest, call 995 or go to A&E. Pause the series until they confirm a doctor has checked them. |
| Fainting (passing out, even briefly) | Stop the session. Call 995 or go to A&E now [25]. Pause the series until they confirm a doctor has checked them. |
| Near-fainting, sudden dizziness or vision change | Stop the session. Sit or lie down. If it does not ease within a few minutes of rest, call 995 or go to A&E. Pause the series until they confirm a doctor has checked them. |
| Breathlessness far beyond what the effort explains, or a racing or irregular heartbeat that does not settle with rest | Stop the session. Sit down. If it does not ease within a few minutes of rest, call 995 or go to A&E. Pause the series until they confirm a doctor has checked them. |
| Sudden sharp pain in a joint or a pop | Stop that exercise. Log a pain flag (E5). Run the symptom check-in; the gate swaps or removes the exercise. |
| A knee giving way or locking | Stop the session. Run the check-in; both are RED flags in `symptomGate.ts`. Suggest assessment. |
| Numbness, tingling or weakness down an arm or leg | Stop the session. Run the check-in; these are RED flags in `symptomGate.ts`. Suggest assessment. |
| A headache that starts suddenly during effort and is extremely painful | Stop the session. Call 995 or go to A&E now [26]. Pause the series until they confirm a doctor has checked them. |
| 1–3 days later: very dark urine with severe, swollen, unusually painful muscles | Suggest same-day medical advice. Pause the series. |
| Normal muscle soreness 1–3 days later | Not a stop sign. The coach says it usually eases within a few days and it is fine to train lightly. |

**Condition flags.** A flag applies the AMBER avoid-set permanently and picks a named path (`paths` in the YAML). A RED check-in on the day adds that region's RED tags on top (`redDaySwaps`). Every swap is named per template session. If a named swap is blocked or already in the session, the library chain decides, skipping ids already there. The script check covers every combination of up to two flags and two RED regions: no session holds the same id twice.

- **Knee or hip flag: "Knee and hip: knee-friendly legs"** (path id `low-impact`). `symptomGate.ts` gives the knee and hip groups the same AMBER set: `impact` and `deep_knee_flexion`.
  - Phase 1 B sessions: `goblet_squat` → `leg_press`. Leg press is then the only squat-pattern lift in both A and B. Watch the leg-press weekly set count rise, and keep its range "moderate", as the library entry says.
  - Phase 2 B2 and Lower 2: `db_split_squat` → `single_leg_press`.
  - Nothing else in the base programme changes. But every squat and single-leg progression (`smith_squat`, `bb_back_squat`, `bulgarian_split_squat`) carries `deep_knee_flexion`, so on this path the only leg progression is adding load on `leg_press` and `single_leg_press`. The path description says so.
  - The path has no `impact` tag, as the PRD requires. The series has no impact moves, so nothing jump-based is lost.
  - Trade-off: the 2026 ACSM stand found full range of motion builds more strength than partial range [2]. A user on this path trades some range for comfort. The copy must not claim the path is identical.
- **Knee RED day** (`knee_load` also goes: `leg_press`, `single_leg_press`, `leg_extension`):

  | Session | Swap |
  |---|---|
  | A (`w1d1`, `w2d2`) and A2 (`w7d1`) | `leg_press` → `hip_thrust` |
  | B (`w1d2`, `w2d1`) | `goblet_squat` (or `leg_press` on a flagged path) → `glute_bridge` |
  | B2 (`w7d2`) and Lower 2 (`w7l2`) | `db_split_squat` (or `single_leg_press`) → `glute_bridge` |
  | Lower 1 (`w7l1`) | `leg_press` → `hip_thrust`; `leg_extension` dropped |

  Dropping `leg_extension` in Lower 1 is a coaching choice. Its chain would give `glute_bridge`, a third hip-extension lift in the session after `hip_thrust` and `db_rdl`. One fewer exercise on a sore-knee day is the better trade.
- **Hip RED day.** It adds `axial_load`, not `knee_load`. In the base programme only `goblet_squat` carries it, and a RED day includes the AMBER set, which already swaps it out. So the leg-press path is unaffected and the session runs as on the low-impact path. Progressions with `axial_load` are never offered.
- **Back flag: "Back-friendly"** (path id `back-friendly`; the same programme with machine-supported squatting). Removes `spinal_flexion` and `axial_load`.
  - Phase 1 B sessions: `goblet_squat` → `leg_press`. Progressions that carry `axial_load` are never offered: `smith_squat`, `bb_back_squat`, `trap_bar_deadlift`, `bb_overhead_press` and `suitcase_carry`.
  - `db_rdl` and `hip_thrust` carry `spinal_load`, which only RED removes. They stay on the flag. That is deliberate: a past back complaint does not ban loaded hinging.
  - The coach cue for `db_rdl` stresses a flat back and stopping where the hamstrings stretch.
- **Back RED day** (`spinal_load` also goes):

  | Session | Swap |
  |---|---|
  | A (`w1d1`, `w2d2`) and A2 (`w7d1`) | `db_rdl` → `seated_leg_curl` |
  | Lower 1 (`w7l1`) | `db_rdl` → `glute_bridge` (`seated_leg_curl` is already in Lower 1) |
  | Lower 2 (`w7l2`) | `hip_thrust` → `glute_bridge` |

- **Neck or shoulder flag: "No overhead"** (path id `no-overhead`). Removes `overhead`; the shoulder region maps to the same set.
  - B, B2 and Upper 1: `db_shoulder_press` → `lateral_raise`. Its chain lists `machine_shoulder_press` first, but that is also `overhead`, so it is skipped. `lateral_raise` is not in B, B2 or Upper 1, so there is no clash.

  - Pressing is still covered by `db_bench_press` and `incline_db_press`.

## 5. In-app intro copy

- **Title:** Gym Strength
- **Promise:** Get stronger in 12 weeks with three gym sessions a week.
- **Description:** A 12-week plan built on a small set of standard gym lifts. You repeat the same lifts and add reps, then weight, as they get easier. Every set stops with reps to spare. Week 6 is lighter so you recover. After that you can stay at three days or move to four.
- **Why it works:**
  - The WHO advises muscle-strengthening work for all major muscles on 2 or more days a week [3]
  - The plan builds towards 10 sets per muscle a week, the level linked to more muscle growth [2]
  - In a 10-week trial, repeating the same leg exercises built as much strength and size as rotating them [14]
- **What you need:** a gym with a leg press, cable machine, dumbbells and a bench. A barbell is optional.
- **Time per week:** about 2.5 hours (3 × 45–55 min); up to about 3.5 hours on the 4-day option.
- **Length:** 12 weeks.

## 8. Claims we will not make

- **"Change your workout often to keep muscles guessing."** Random, frequent rotation may hurt gains [13], and a planned rotation gave no extra benefit over repeating the same lifts [14].
- **"Light weights for high reps make you lean; heavy weights make you bulky."** Size gains were similar across loads from 30% to 100% of max [2]. Average gains across 111 studies were about 1.5 kg of lean mass, measured only in men [22].
- **"Work an area to lose fat from that area."** Not covered by any source we reviewed. Fat loss is not a goal of this series, and the copy will not mention it.
- **"You must train to failure to grow."** Not needed; 2–3 reps in reserve is enough [2][9].
- **"No pain, no gain" / "soreness means it worked."** We have not reviewed soreness research. The app tracks reps and load, not soreness, and never praises pain.
- **"Add X kg of muscle in 12 weeks."** Response varies from almost nothing to a lot [21]. No number goes in copy.
- **"You need a periodised plan (or a deload) to make progress."** Periodisation did not consistently change outcomes [2]; a full week off reduced lower-body strength gains in one trial [18]. Our easier week is a convention [19], and we say so.
- **"Barbells are better than machines."** Equipment type did not consistently change outcomes [2].
- **"This programme is safe for everyone."** It is safe for most healthy adults [2], but §4 lists who should check first.
