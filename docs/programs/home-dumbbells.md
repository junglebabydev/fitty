# Home Dumbbells — research and programme (Oct 2026)

**Verdict.** A pair of adjustable dumbbells is enough to build muscle, provided sets end close to failure. Light and heavy loads give similar growth in meta-analyses when sets are taken to failure [3][4], and ACSM's 2026 overview of 137 systematic reviews found equipment type (mainly machines versus free weights) did not consistently change outcomes [1]. Two limits: heavy loads still win for maximal strength [3][4], and we found no trial of a home dumbbell programme in healthy adults under 60 (the home-based evidence is in older adults and small pilots [16][17]). A common mistake: a light dumbbell for a comfortable 3 × 12 far from failure. In one trial, light sets stopped well short of failure did not grow muscle significantly, while light sets to failure did [5].

## 1. Who it is for, and who should check first

- **For:**
  - Adults who want to get stronger and build muscle at home.
  - Owners of one pair of adjustable dumbbells, whatever the top weight. The plan assumes the dumbbells will run out of load for legs and hips, and has rules for that.
  - Beginners, and people returning after a break. People who have lifted regularly for 3 months or more start at week 3.
  - Anyone who has 30–40 minutes three times a week and a 2 × 2 m patch of floor.
- **Not for, or talk to a clinician first:**
  - Chest pain, fainting, palpitations or unusual breathlessness on effort. Do not start; see a clinician.
  - Known heart, metabolic or kidney disease, and not currently active. Get clearance before starting. Current activity, symptoms or known disease, and intended intensity are the three factors ACSM uses to decide on clearance [18].
  - Pregnant. Do not start this series; talk to your midwife or clinician first.
  - Had a baby in the last 12 months (a coaching-convention cut-off). Use the Postpartum series instead, unless you have already finished it.
  - Surgery, a fracture or a joint injury in the last 3 months.
  - Aged 65 or over. The series covers strength, but WHO also advises varied activity that emphasises balance and strength on 3 or more days a week at this age [2]. The app shows a note to add balance work, because this series alone does not meet that advice.
  - Anyone chasing a 1-rep-max goal, such as a powerlifting total. Light loads build less maximal strength [3][4]. The Traditional Gym series fits that goal.

**Dose.** WHO sets the floor: all major muscle groups, at moderate or greater intensity, on 2 or more days a week [2]. ACSM's 2026 position stand sets the next step: about 10 or more sets per muscle per week for hypertrophy, and 2–3 sets per exercise for strength [1]. Older meta-analyses agree that more weekly sets give more growth [9]. The 2026 meta-regressions add that returns diminish, more steeply for strength than for size [10]. This series starts at 2 sets per exercise (weeks 1–2). By weeks 7–10 it reaches 9–12 direct sets a week for quads, hips and back. Chest and shoulders get 5–7 direct sets plus indirect work from presses and push-ups. In [10], counting each indirect set as half a set was the model that fitted best; it is a counting rule, not a measured effect. That is below the 10-set mark for those muscles, a trade made to keep sessions under 40 minutes.

**Frequency.** At equal weekly volume, how often you train a muscle has little effect on size [11]. Strength rises with frequency, with diminishing returns [10]. Three full-body sessions spread the sets thinly, so each session stays short. ACSM's strength finding is 2 or more sessions a week [1]. Three is a comfortable margin.

**Intensity and effort.** This is the core of a home series. Across 21 studies, light (≤60% 1RM) and heavy loads gave similar hypertrophy when every set went to failure [3]. A network meta-analysis of 28 studies found the same, with heavy and moderate loads better than light ones for strength [4]. The catch is effort. In one 8-week trial of one-leg knee extensions in untrained men, light sets to failure grew the quadriceps by about 8%. Volume-matched light sets stopped well short of failure grew it by about 3%, which was not significant [5]. An exploratory meta-regression found growth rises as sets end closer to failure, while strength gains were similar across a wide range of reps in reserve [7]. Going all the way to momentary failure is not needed: it did not consistently change outcomes in ACSM's overview [1], and was not better than stopping short in a meta-analysis of 15 studies [6]. So for heavy-enough sets the series aims at 1–2 reps in reserve. Once the load is capped, it aims at 0–1, with the last set taken to technical failure. People misjudge reps left by about 1, and misjudge more above 12 reps [8]. That is why the high-rep stage uses a measured last set, not a guess.

**Progression.** Double progression, already in `src/engine/progression.ts`: reach the top of the rep range on every set, then add the smallest dumbbell step. When the next step is above the heaviest dumbbell, the series switches to longer sets of 15–30 reps at that load. This is supported by the load data [3][4]. The cap of 30 is a coaching convention, to stop sets dragging past about 90 seconds. When 30 is reached, the exercise moves to a harder variant. One-leg versions roughly double the load per leg. One-limb and two-limb training gave similar hypertrophy in 9 studies [15], so the swap is not expected to cost size, though the evidence is limited. A slower tempo (3 s down, 1 s pause) makes a light weight harder. It does not add growth by itself: durations from 0.5 to 8 s give similar results, and very slow reps may be worse [14]. Treat tempo as a way to reach failure with fewer reps. One cost to name: ACSM found a full range of motion improves strength [1], and three moves here use a shortened range (`goblet_squat` to chair height, the short-range `db_split_squat`, `db_floor_press`). We accept that for joint comfort and for a set-up with no bench; with a bench, `db_bench_press` restores the range.

**Home-specific evidence, honestly.** We found no RCT of a home dumbbell programme in healthy adults under 60. The closest evidence:
- Unsupervised home programmes in adults aged 60+ improved leg strength modestly (g = 0.33), with no major adverse events, and adherence averaged 67% [16].
- A 12-week pilot RCT in adults with overweight or obesity on a weight-loss diet used bands and bodyweight 3 times a week. Grip strength, knee-extensor torque and sit-to-stand improved. Muscle thickness did not differ from diet alone [17].
- Bands match conventional weights for strength [12], and a band push-up matched the bench press over 5 weeks [13].

The case for this series rests on load equivalence [3][4], the ACSM equipment finding [1] and these trials. It does not rest on a home dumbbell trial.

**Expected results and timescale.** The trials behind these claims ran 5 weeks to 12 months [1][13]. Strength usually shows first, as more reps or more load from week to week. Size changes are smaller and slower: about 8% in the quadriceps after 8 weeks of light sets to failure in untrained men, in one trial [5]. Copy should promise "stronger, measured in your log", not a look.

## 3. The programme

- **Length:** 10 weeks. **Sessions:** 3 a week, full body, at least one rest day between sessions. **Minutes:** about 30 (weeks 1–2), 35 (weeks 3–6), 40 (weeks 7–10), including a 4-minute warm-up.
- **Equipment:** one pair of adjustable dumbbells. Optional: a flat bench or sturdy chair, and a resistance band. Floor space about 2 × 2 m.
- **Session shape:** `sets` (sets × reps, plus timed sets for side plank and carry), after one `steady` warm-up block: 4 minutes of `march_in_place`. There are no intervals or circuits. Rest periods are part of the prescription.
- **Phases:**
  - Weeks 1–2, *Learn*: 2 sets, 3 reps in reserve, 8–12 reps.
  - Weeks 3–6, *Build*: 3 sets, 2 in reserve, 8–15 reps.
  - Weeks 7–10, *Push*: the first two exercises go to 4 sets, 1 in reserve.
- **Sessions:**
  - A: squat and horizontal press.
  - B: hinge and split squat.
  - C: step-up, hip thrust and overhead press.
  - All three include a one-arm row. The row works with a hand on a chair, so it needs no bench.

**Time check (weeks 7–10, the longest).** One-side sets count twice. A ≈ 39 min, B ≈ 39 min, C ≈ 39 min. Each total is the 4-minute warm-up plus sets × (work + rest).

## 4. Safety

**Pre-start screen.** Six questions. Each "yes" maps to one outcome from PRD §4.4. These are our rules, built on ACSM's three screening factors [18]; they are not ACSM's own decision tree. Training history is not a safety question, so it is asked at setup (rule P0), not here.

| Question | Outcome | If yes, the app… |
|---|---|---|
| 1. In the last 3 months, have you had chest pain, fainting, palpitations or unusual breathlessness when active? | `wait` | Does not start the series. It asks the user to see a clinician first and shows nothing to train. |
| 2. Do you have a diagnosed heart, metabolic or kidney condition, and are you currently inactive? | `wait` | Asks the user to get clearance first. The Start button unlocks only after they confirm clearance. |
| 3. Are you pregnant? | `wait` | Does not start this series. It asks the user to talk to their midwife or clinician first. |
| 4. Have you had a baby in the last 12 months? | `suggest:postpartum` | Points to the Postpartum series, which has its own clearance step. **Stop signs during or after a session.**

| Sign | What to do |
|---|---|
| Chest pain or pressure, fainting or near-fainting, a racing or irregular heartbeat, or breathlessness out of proportion to the effort | Stop now and sit or lie down. Do not resume today. If chest pain lasts more than a few minutes, call emergency services. The app ends the session and locks the series until the user confirms they have seen a clinician. |
| Sharp or joint pain, as opposed to the burn of a hard set | Stop that exercise and log a pain flag. The gate swaps or removes it for the rest of the session. Progression holds (engine). |
| Numbness, tingling or pain spreading down an arm or leg; a knee locking or giving way | Stop the session. Gate RED applies to that region. The app suggests an assessment before the next session. |
| Severe muscle pain and swelling with dark, cola-coloured urine in the days after a session | Seek same-day medical care. The app pauses the series. (Safety practice, not from the sources above.) |
| Dizziness when standing up from the floor | Stand up slowly and sit before the next set. If it keeps happening, stop and check with a clinician. |

**Condition flags.** These are standing flags, so the AMBER tag set applies every session. Every swap below is home-only. The library's own substitution chains point at gym machines (`leg_press`, `single_leg_press`, `machine_shoulder_press`), so the series must override them.

- **Knee or hip flag: the low-impact path (path id `low-impact`).** For both regions, AMBER removes `deep_knee_flexion` and `impact`. That takes out `goblet_squat` and `db_split_squat`.
  - Session A: `goblet_squat` → `wall_sit`, 3 × 30–60 s, thighs above parallel (about 45–60 degrees of knee bend). The depth is a coaching convention that keeps the knee out of deep bending, which this path removes.
  - Session B: `db_split_squat` → `db_step_up` on a low step.
  - Session C: keeps `db_step_up`.
  - Hinge, press, row and core are unchanged.
  - Quad work moves from three exercises (squat, split squat, step-up) to one wall sit and two step-ups. The set count is the same, but the knee bends less and the wall sit is a hold, so expect smaller quad gains. The app says so.
  - The capped-load ladder for step-ups is tempo only. `bulgarian_split_squat` and `deficit_reverse_lunge` are tagged `deep_knee_flexion`, so they are not on this path.
  - A RED knee day also removes `knee_load`, which takes out `wall_sit` and `db_step_up`. It becomes a hips-and-upper-body day: `db_rdl`, `hip_thrust` or `glute_bridge`, press, row and core.
  - A RED hip day adds `axial_load` instead, not `knee_load`. `goblet_squat` is already gone, so it takes out `suitcase_carry`: Session C swaps it for `side_plank`, as on the back path. Wall sit and step-ups stay.
- **Back flag: the back-friendly path (path id `back`).** AMBER removes `axial_load` and `spinal_flexion`. That takes out `goblet_squat` and `suitcase_carry`.
  - Session A: `goblet_squat` → `db_step_up`.
  - Session C: `suitcase_carry` → `side_plank`.
  - `db_rdl` and `hip_thrust` (tagged `spinal_load`) stay on the plan at AMBER, and so do their ladder rungs. The app says so plainly rather than implying the hinge is gone.
  - A RED back day removes `spinal_load`: `db_rdl` → `glute_bridge` and `hip_thrust` → `glute_bridge`.
- **Knee (or hip) and back flags together** (the demo persona). Both AMBER sets apply at once, removing `impact`, `deep_knee_flexion`, `axial_load` and `spinal_flexion`.
  - Session A: `goblet_squat` → `wall_sit`, thighs above parallel.
  - Session B: `db_split_squat` → `db_step_up`.
  - Session C: `db_step_up` stays; `suitcase_carry` → `side_plank`.
  - `db_rdl` and `hip_thrust` stay. `dead_bug`, `side_plank`, presses and rows carry none of the removed tags.
  - The leg ladder is tempo only, because `bulgarian_split_squat` and `deficit_reverse_lunge` are tagged `deep_knee_flexion`.
- **Neck or shoulder flag: the no-overhead path (path id `no-overhead`).** AMBER removes `overhead`.
  - Session C: `db_shoulder_press` → `lateral_raise`, 3 × 12–20.
  - `arnold_press` is also excluded from the ladder.
  - Nothing else in the series is tagged `overhead`. A RED neck day also removes `neck_load`, which nothing else in the series carries.

## 5. In-app intro copy

- **Title:** Home Dumbbells
- **Promise:** Get stronger at home with one pair of dumbbells.
- **Description:** Ten weeks, three full-body sessions a week, 30 to 40 minutes each. You need one pair of adjustable dumbbells; a chair or bench and a band help but are optional. When your dumbbells stop being heavy enough, the plan adds reps, then harder one-leg or slower versions, so you keep progressing.
- **Why it works:**
  - Light and heavy weights built similar muscle when sets were taken to failure. [3]
  - In one 8-week trial, light sets stopped well short of failure grew little muscle. [5]
  - In ACSM's 2026 overview, equipment type, such as machines versus free weights, did not consistently change results. [1]
- **What you need:** one pair of adjustable dumbbells. Optional: a bench or sturdy chair, a resistance band. Floor space about 2 × 2 m.
- **Time per week:** about 1 hour 30 to 2 hours (3 sessions of 30–40 minutes).
- **Length:** 10 weeks.

## 8. Claims we will not make

- **"Light weights are for definition, heavy weights are for bulk."** Light and heavy loads give similar growth when every set goes to failure [3][4]. None of the sources here supports the definition-versus-bulk idea.
- **"High reps take fat off the area you train."** No source here supports targeted fat loss. Training a muscle grows that muscle; it does not choose where fat comes off.
- **"Slow reps build more muscle."** Durations of 0.5–8 s give similar growth, and very slow reps may be worse [14]. Tempo is a tool for a light weight, not a bonus.
- **"Train to failure on every set."** Momentary failure was not better than stopping short [6], and ACSM found it did not consistently change outcomes [1]. The capped stage takes only the last set to technical failure.
- **"Home dumbbells match a gym for everything."** Hypertrophy, probably, if sets go to or near failure [1][3][4]. Maximal strength, no: heavy loads win [3][4].
- **"One-leg work is better than two-leg work."** Hypertrophy was similar in 9 studies [15]. We use one-leg work to make a light dumbbell heavy, not because it is better.
- **"Change your exercises often to keep muscles guessing."** None of the sources here supports it. The series repeats the same movements and progresses them.
- **"Visible results in 2 weeks."** Trials run 5 weeks or more [1][13]. Quadriceps size rose about 8% after 8 weeks in one trial [5]. We promise progress in the log, not a look.
- **"Proven by home-workout trials."** We found no RCT of home dumbbell training in healthy adults under 60. The home trials we have are in older adults [16] or are small pilots [17].
