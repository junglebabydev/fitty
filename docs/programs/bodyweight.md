# Bodyweight Anywhere — research and programme (Oct 2026)

**Verdict.** Light loads build muscle when sets are taken to or near failure [6][7][9], so this series progresses by harder variants rather than added load. The evidence is moderate for the push-up (four small studies in young adults, mostly men, upper-body strength outcomes only, 4–8 weeks [1][2][3][5]) and thin elsewhere: the squat, hinge and row ladders are coaching convention. A common pattern in this genre is to add reps forever ("100 push-ups a day"). At light loads, one trial found effort mattered more than total volume for growth [7], and growth rises as sets end nearer failure [9]. Heavy loads still build more maximal strength than light loads [6][8], and the app says so.

## 1. Who it is for, and who should check first

- **For:**
  - adults who train at home, in a hotel room or outdoors, with no equipment;
  - beginners up to people who can do about 15 clean floor push-ups;
  - anyone who wants 3 short sessions a week (20–30 min);
  - travellers who need the same programme in any room with a floor, a chair, and a heavy table or a solid door.
- **Not for, or talk to a clinician first:**
  - Chest pain, fainting or unusual breathlessness during exertion, or a known heart, kidney or metabolic condition with symptoms. Get clearance before starting. ACSM screening uses signs or symptoms, known heart, metabolic or kidney disease, current activity and intended intensity to decide who needs clearance [14]; the symptom examples are ours.
  - Pregnant. Do not start this series; talk to your midwife or doctor first.
  - Had a baby in the last 12 months (a coaching-convention cut-off). Use the Postpartum series first, unless you have already finished it.
  - Surgery on a shoulder, wrist, hip, knee or the spine in the last 3 months. Get clearance from the surgeon or physio first.
  - Shoulder or wrist pain when taking weight on the hands. The push ladder is capped (see §4).
  - Back pain that travels down a leg, or new numbness or weakness in a leg. Do not start; see a clinician.
  - Goal is a maximal lift such as a heavier bench or squat. This series will not get you there. Use Traditional Gym [6][8].
  - Already doing 15+ archer push-ups and Bulgarian split squats per side. This series is outgrown; use Home Dumbbells or Traditional Gym.

## 2. The evidence

| Claim | Grade | Source [n] | Use in app? |

**Strength, upper body.** In two randomised trials, push-ups did as well as the bench press when matched for muscle activity or load [1][2]. Calatayud loaded push-ups with elastic bands to a 6-rep max, matched to the bench press by muscle activity (EMG); 1RM and 6RM rose similarly in 5 weeks in 30 university students with advanced training experience [1]. Kikuchi used kneeling or adjusted push-ups at the same load as a 40% 1RM bench, twice a week for 8 weeks, in 18 men. 1RM and chest and triceps thickness rose in both groups [2]. Kotarsky progressed the push-up by variation, 3 days a week for 4 weeks, in 23 men. 1RM bench rose in both the push-up and the bench group, and muscle thickness did not change in either [3]. Of these four studies, that is the only test of "progress by harder variation". All four were in young adults (three in men only), measured upper-body strength only, and had 30 or fewer people [1][2][3][5].

**Load ladder.** Ebben measured peak vertical ground reaction force in six push-up variants in 23 recreationally fit men and women [4]. Feet raised was the hardest. Hands raised and knees bent were the easiest. This is the one ladder in the series with a measured order. The squat, bridge and row ladders follow the same logic: shorter lever, one leg instead of two, more of the body horizontal. They have not been tested as ladders, so they are labelled coaching convention.

**Intensity and effort.** In studies where every set went to failure, light loads grew muscle as well as heavy loads [6]. Lasevicius gave each leg of 25 untrained men a different knee-extension protocol for 8 weeks, two of them at 30% 1RM. At 30%, sets to failure grew the quadriceps about as much as heavy loads did. Sets stopped well short of failure did not grow it significantly [7]. Robinson's exploratory meta-regression found the same direction: growth improves as sets end nearer failure, while strength gains are similar across a wide range [9]. The 2026 ACSM position stand found that training to momentary muscle fatigue did not consistently change outcomes [8]. The rules are consistent with both findings. ACSM's finding covers resistance training in general. Lasevicius isolates light loads. Our reasoning, not tested: bodyweight becomes a light load for most movements once a person adapts. So the series asks for 1–2 reps in reserve (close, not to failure) and moves to a harder variant when the reps climb. That keeps the load in a range where 1–2 in reserve still means hard work.

**Dose and frequency.** WHO recommends muscle-strengthening work on 2+ days a week for all major muscle groups [10]. ACSM 2026 adds: at least 2 sessions a week and 2–3 sets for strength, and 10 or more sets a week for growth [8]. This series gives 3 sessions a week. From week 3, push, squat, pull and hinge each get 6 hard sets a week on the two sets days; core gets 4. The 3–4 circuit rounds run one rung down (P7), so they are not hard sets. The weekly total of hard sets is below the 10 a week that ACSM links with more growth. That is a sound floor, not a growth maximum, as TRAINING_EVIDENCE.md already notes for 3×/week.

**Variation.** Changing exercises systematically, by anatomy and biomechanics, may help. Excessive, random rotation may hinder gains [11]. The review covered 8 studies, all in young men. We treat one ladder per movement, climbed in order, as systematic variation; the review did not test ladders.

**What to expect, and when.** Kotarsky measured strength gains at 4 weeks [3]. Kikuchi measured chest and triceps thickness gains at 8 weeks [2]. Thomas's 8-week programme raised push-up test results by 16% and pull-up test results by 39% in untrained men, with no change in handgrip [5]. Copy should say: "most people move up at least one rung on each ladder in 8 weeks". That matches the rule design. It is not a measured outcome. No source here supports a promise about body fat or appearance.

## 3. The programme

- **Length:** 8 weeks, 3 sessions a week (24 sessions).
- **Time:** sets days take up to 22 minutes in weeks 1–2, and up to 30 minutes from week 3, when per-side rungs double the time per set. Circuit days take 20–25 minutes.
- **Equipment:** none to buy. You need a floor, a sturdy chair or sofa edge, and either a heavy table or a solid door with a towel. A bar (`inverted_row`) is optional.
- **Session shapes:**
  - Day 1 and Day 3: **sets** (sets × reps, or sets × seconds for holds).
  - Day 2: **circuit** (5 stations × rounds, timed).
  - No intervals or steady sessions.
- **Slots and ladders.** Each session has fixed slots: push, squat, pull, hinge and core. Each slot has a ladder of exercise ids. The `exerciseId` written in a session is the **starting rung**. At run time the app replaces it with the user's current rung in that ladder (see `progression`).

### Ladders (by exercise id)

| Slot | Rung 1 | Rung 2 | Rung 3 | Rung 4 | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| push | `incline_push_up` | `push_up` | `decline_push_up` | `archer_push_up` (per side) | Measured load order for rungs 1–3 [4]; rung 4 is convention |
| squat | `bodyweight_squat` | `bw_split_squat` (per side) | `bulgarian_split_squat` (per side) | none in this series | Convention |
| squat, **knee and hip path** | `sit_to_stand_chair` (new) | `step_up_bw` (new, per side) | none | none | Convention |
| pull | `towel_door_row` (new) | `table_inverted_row` (new) | `inverted_row` (only with a bar) | none | Convention |
| hinge (bridge) | `glute_bridge` | `single_leg_glute_bridge` (per side) | `single_leg_hip_thrust` (new, per side) | none | Convention |
| hinge (standing) | `single_leg_rdl_bw` (per side) | none | none | none | Single rung; progress by reps then 3-s lowering |
| core A | `dead_bug` | `plank` | none | none | Convention |
| core B | `side_plank` (per side) | `side_plank_reach` (per side) | none | none | Convention |
| circuit conditioning | `mountain_climber` | none | none | none | Single rung |

Each rung carries its own rep or seconds range (see `ladders` in the YAML), so a slot's range changes when its rung changes.

With no table and no door, `prone_y_t_raise` (new) stands in for the pull slot. It is **not** equivalent to a row: no pulling load on the arms, only the upper back. The app says so on screen.

### Week-by-week overview

| Week | Day 1 (sets A) | Day 2 (circuit) | Day 3 (sets B) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 sets per slot | 3 rounds, 30 s on / 30 s off | 2 sets per slot | Placement week: can jump one rung per set |
| 2 | 2 sets | 3 rounds, 30/30 | 2 sets | Same as week 1 |
| 3 | 3 sets | 3 rounds, 40/20 | 3 sets | Volume step |
| 4 | 3 sets | 3 rounds, 40/20 | 3 sets | Same as week 3 |
| 5 | 3 sets | 4 rounds, 40/20 | 3 sets | Circuit volume step |
| 6 | 3 sets | 4 rounds, 40/20 | 3 sets | Same as week 5 |
| 7 | 3 sets | 4 rounds, 40/20 | 3 sets | Same as week 5 |
| 8 | 3 sets | 4 rounds, 40/20 | 3 sets | App compares rungs and reps with week 1 |

Load does not rise by changing the template. It rises when a slot moves up a rung, which can happen in any week.

### Minutes, worked out

The minutes are upper estimates for a user whose slots are all on their longest rung. They assume 3 s per rep, both sides counted on per-side moves, 10 s to switch sides, and 30 s between exercises. On the starting rungs, sessions run about 4 minutes shorter.

- **Day 1 (sets A), weeks 1–2 (2 sets):**
  - 2 min warm-up;
  - push 3.4 min (`archer_push_up` 4–8 per side);
  - squat 4.2 min (`bw_split_squat` 8–12 per side);
  - pull 3 min (`towel_door_row` 8–15);
  - hinge 4 min (single-leg rung);
  - core 3.3 min (`dead_bug` per side);
  - total 19.9 min. The template shows 22.
- **Day 3 (sets B), weeks 1–2:**
  - the same slots, with the standing hinge at 4 min and `side_plank` per side at 4 min;
  - total 20.6 min. The template shows 22.
- **Day 1, weeks 3–8 (3 sets):**
  - 2 min warm-up;
  - push 5.4 min;
  - squat 6.6 min;
  - pull 4.8 min;
  - hinge 6.1 min;
  - core 3.3 min;
  - total 28.1 min. The template shows 30.
- **Day 3, weeks 3–8:**
  - 2 + 4.8 + 6.6 + 5.4 + 6.1 (`single_leg_rdl_bw`) + 4 (`side_plank`);
  - total 28.9 min. The template shows 30.
- **Day 2:**
  - one round is 5 × 60 s, with 60 s between rounds;
  - 3 rounds come to 17 min, plus a 2 min warm-up: 20 min shown;
  - 4 rounds come to 23 min, plus a 2 min warm-up: 25 min shown.

## 4. Safety

### Pre-start screen

| # | Question (yes / no) | If yes, the app… |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Has a doctor said you have a heart condition, or do you get chest pain, fainting or unusual breathlessness when you exert yourself? | Does not start the series. Shows "Please get clearance from your doctor first" and keeps the series locked until the user confirms clearance [14]. |
| 2 | Are you pregnant? | Does not start this series (`wait`). Shows "Please talk to your midwife or doctor about exercise in pregnancy." |
| 2b | Have you had a baby in the last 12 months? | Offers the Postpartum series instead (`suggest:postpartum`), unless the user's Postpartum enrolment has status `done`; then the answer is a `note` and the series starts. The 12-month cut-off is a coaching convention. |
| 3 | Have you had surgery, or an injury that needed treatment, in the last 3 months? | Does not start. Asks the user to get clearance from their surgeon or physio, then confirm. |
| 4 | Do your knees or hips hurt on stairs or when squatting, or do you have a knee or hip condition flag? | Starts the series on the **knee and hip path**: squat slot uses `sit_to_stand_chair` → `step_up_bw`. |
| 5 | Do your shoulders or wrists hurt when you take weight on your hands? | Caps the push ladder at `push_up`, so no `decline_push_up` or `archer_push_up`. Swaps `mountain_climber` for `dead_bug` in the circuit. Starts the push slot at rung 1 even in placement week. |
| 6 | Do you have back pain that travels down a leg, or new numbness or weakness in a leg? | Does not start. Shows "This needs a clinician's check before training". |

### Stop signs

| During or after a session | What the app tells the user |
|---|---|
| Chest pain or pressure, fainting or near-fainting, a racing or irregular heartbeat, breathlessness out of proportion to the effort | "Stop now and sit down. If it does not settle within a few minutes, call emergency services." Ends the session and locks the series until the user confirms they have seen a doctor. |
| Sharp or pinching joint pain (not the burn of effort) | "Stop this exercise." Logs a pain tap: P4 drops a rung and the symptom check opens. |
| Pain that is worse the next day and still worse at the next session | Runs the symptom check before the session. The symptom gate decides the substitutions. |
| Severe muscle pain with swelling, or very dark urine, in the days after a session | "Please get medical advice today." Pauses the series. Coaching convention, no source here: watch hardest in the first week, after many high-rep sets to near failure. |
| Dizziness in a plank or bridge | "Breathe out on effort and don't hold your breath." If it repeats, the app treats it as a stop sign in row 1. |

### Condition flags

Gate rule (`src/engine/symptomGate.ts`): a standing flag applies the AMBER avoid set permanently.

- **Knee** (AMBER avoids `impact` and `deep_knee_flexion`):
  - The series has **no impact moves at all**, so nothing is lost there.
  - The deep-flexion moves are removed: `bodyweight_squat`, `bw_split_squat` and `bulgarian_split_squat`. Every other slot is unchanged.
  - The squat slot runs the **knee path**: `sit_to_stand_chair` (seat at or above knee height) → `step_up_bw` (step no higher than 20 cm). Both are tagged `knee_load` only.
  - **Knee RED day:** RED adds `knee_load`, so the knee path is removed for that day. The squat slot becomes `side_lying_hip_abduction` (2–3 × [12, 20] per side) and the hinge slot holds at `glute_bridge`.
  - Honest line for the intro: "Your knee path builds the legs more slowly than the full ladder."
- **Hip** (AMBER avoids `impact` and `deep_knee_flexion`; RED adds `axial_load`):
  - The same three deep-flexion squat moves are removed, so the squat slot runs the same path as for knees: `sit_to_stand_chair` → `step_up_bw`.
  - Hip RED adds only `axial_load`, which nothing here carries, so the path stays on a hip RED day. The `squatKneeRed` swap is for knee RED only.
  - The hinge and core slots are unchanged; a pain tap there still drops a rung (P4).
  - Honest line for the intro: "Your hip path builds the legs more slowly than the full ladder."
- **Back** (AMBER avoids `spinal_flexion` and `axial_load`; RED adds `spinal_load`):
  - No exercise in this series carries any of these tags, so the gate removes nothing.
  - The series still respects pain taps: if `plank` or `single_leg_rdl_bw` provokes back pain, P4 drops the core slot to `dead_bug`, and the standing hinge to `glute_bridge` for that session.
- **Neck** (AMBER avoids `overhead`; RED adds `neck_load`):
  - No exercise in this series carries either tag, which is why vertical pushing and pulling were left out.
  - `prone_y_t_raise` is done with the forehead resting on a folded towel, so the neck carries no load.

## 5. In-app intro copy

- **Title:** Bodyweight Anywhere
- **Promise:** Get stronger in any room, with nothing to carry.
- **Description:** Three short sessions a week using only your body, a chair and a table or door. Each movement has a ladder of harder versions. When your reps get easy, you move up a rung, so the work stays hard enough to count. Two strength days and one circuit day, for 8 weeks.
- **Why it works:**
  - In a small trial, push-ups matched to a light bench press built similar strength and muscle in 8 weeks [2]
  - With light loads, sets to failure built muscle; sets stopped far short did not, in one trial [7]
  - WHO recommends strength work for all major muscle groups on two or more days a week [10]
- **What you need:** a floor, a sturdy chair, and a heavy table or a solid door with a towel.
- **Time per week:** about 60–85 minutes (3 sessions of 20–30 minutes).
- **Length:** 8 weeks.
- **One honest line (shown under "Why it works"):** Heavy weights still win for maximal strength. This series builds a strong base.

**`bw_split_squat` / `bulgarian_split_squat`**

- "Whole foot on the step, drive up."
- "Step down slowly, no dropping."

- "Pull your chest to your hands."
- "Lower for a slow count of two."

**`glute_bridge` / `single_leg_glute_bridge` / `single_leg_hip_thrust`**
- "Drive through the heel, squeeze at the top."
- "Ribs down, hips level."

**`single_leg_rdl_bw`**
- "Soft knee, hinge at the hip."
- "Stop when the back of the thigh stretches."

**`dead_bug` / `plank` / `side_plank`**
- "Lower back stays heavy on the floor."
- "Breathe out as the arm and leg reach."
- "Straight line, breathe, quality seconds only."

**`march_in_place`**
- "Easy pace. Knees up, arms swinging."

**`mountain_climber`**
- "Shoulders over hands, hips stay low."

**Rests**
- "Rest now. Shake out your arms."
- "Next up in ten seconds."
- "How many were left in the tank?"

**Moving up or down a rung**
- "Every set hit the top. Next time, harder version."
- "Same version today. That is how strength builds."
- "We dropped a rung so the form stays clean."

**Finish**
- "Done. Three sessions a week is the whole trick."
- "Nice work. Log how it felt while it's fresh."

## 8. Claims we will not make

- **"Bodyweight builds as much strength as lifting weights."** Light loads match heavy loads for muscle size, not for maximal strength [6][7][8].
- **"Push-ups are as good as bench press."** That held only when matched for muscle activity or load. The trials were small, 4–8 weeks, and in young adults, mostly men [1][2][3].
- **"Improves your posture."** Thomas's authors report a posture result from tests with eyes open and eyes closed, in one small study [5]. We could not open the methods to see what it measures, so we do not claim it.
- **"Burns fat" or "changes your body shape."** The only body-fat data here is one small study of 28 men across training and control groups, in which fat mass fell in the training group [5]. We could not confirm how fat mass was measured. The series is not a fat-loss programme.
- **"100 push-ups a day" or rep challenges.** At light loads, sets stopped well short of failure did not grow muscle significantly in one trial [7], and growth rises as sets end nearer failure [9].
- **"Five minutes a day is enough to build muscle."** Exercise snacks improved aerobic fitness, but showed no lower-limb strength or body-composition effect [12].
- **"Results in 7 days."** The earliest measured strength change in these trials was at 4 weeks [3].
- **"Calisthenics is safer than weights."** We found no comparative injury data for home bodyweight training.
- **"Targets", "tones" or "sculpts" a body part.** Banned wording. We have no source on spot reduction in this document and make no claim either way.
